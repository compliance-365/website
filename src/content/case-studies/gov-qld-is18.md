## The situation

A Brisbane-based managed services provider (MSP) with 45 staff had held a Queensland Government ICT supplier panel position for six years. Their primary revenue stream was managing IT infrastructure for three Queensland state government agencies — covering end-user compute, network operations, and service desk for a combined user base of approximately 800 government staff.

In mid-2025, their panel coordinator notified them that panel renewal would require demonstrated compliance with the Queensland Government's Information Standard IS18 (Information Security) and evidence of Essential Eight maturity at ML1 or above, with a documented pathway to ML2.

They had six weeks before their renewal submission deadline. They had no formal IS18 compliance assessment, and their last Essential Eight self-assessment — conducted internally 18 months prior — was based on a spreadsheet that the team themselves acknowledged was optimistic.

The stakes were significant: the three government agency contracts represented approximately 65% of total revenue.

## What IS18 requires — and what it means for ICT suppliers

Understanding IS18 is essential context for any Queensland Government ICT supplier. Here's what it actually means in practice.

### IS18 and the Queensland Government ICT security framework

Queensland Government Information Standard 18 (IS18) is the Queensland Government's mandatory information security standard. It applies directly to all Queensland Government agencies — but it also flows down to ICT suppliers through contract clauses and panel requirements.

IS18 establishes requirements across four key areas:

**1. Information security governance**
Agencies (and their suppliers) must maintain a documented information security management programme. This means: a current information security policy approved by senior leadership, defined security roles and responsibilities, a risk management methodology, and evidence of regular management review of the security programme. IS18 aligns this requirement to ISO 27001 principles — but it doesn't require ISO 27001 certification. An IS18-compliant governance programme is a pragmatic, documented alternative.

**2. Essential Eight implementation**
IS18 explicitly mandates the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies. For Queensland Government agencies, the target is ML2 across all eight controls. For ICT suppliers accessing government systems or handling government data, IS18-compliance requirements are passed through procurement — and increasingly, panel coordinators are asking suppliers to evidence Essential Eight maturity directly rather than accepting a self-assessment checkbox.

**3. Information classification and handling**
IS18 requires a documented information classification scheme aligned to the Queensland Government's classification framework (which maps to the Australian Government's classification scheme). Suppliers handling government data must demonstrate that their systems handle classified and sensitive information appropriately — this includes data at rest, data in transit, and data in cloud or third-party environments.

**4. Incident management and business continuity**
IS18 requires a documented incident response capability, including tested procedures, defined escalation paths, and — critically — notification obligations to the Queensland Government Chief Information Officer (QGCIO) for serious incidents. Suppliers must have incident response plans that cover government-related incidents, including how they would notify the agency client within defined timeframes.

### What ICT supplier IS18 compliance actually looks like

This is where many suppliers get confused. IS18 is an agency standard — it doesn't publish a "supplier checklist." What it does is establish requirements that Queensland Government agencies must apply when procuring ICT services. In practice, this means:

- **Panel qualification submissions** require IS18 compliance evidence — typically an IS18 Compliance Statement, Essential Eight maturity evidence, and a current security policy
- **Contract schedules** include IS18 security obligations that flow to the supplier
- **Security questionnaires** issued during procurement reference IS18 controls directly
- **Panel renewals** are increasingly being used to re-validate supplier compliance rather than assume it persists

The IS18 Compliance Statement is the document that most panel coordinators and procurement teams actually read. It should summarise your current security posture, map your controls to IS18 requirements, identify any gaps, and commit to a remediation timeline. A well-constructed IS18 Compliance Statement — even one that acknowledges current gaps with a credible plan — is substantially better than a vague self-attestation.

### IS18 vs ISO 27001: Do you need both?

A question we hear frequently from government ICT suppliers. The short answer:

- **ISO 27001 certification satisfies IS18's governance requirements** — if you hold a current ISO 27001 certificate, you have documented evidence of an ISMS that meets IS18's information security management programme requirements. You still need to evidence Essential Eight separately, as it's an ASD framework rather than an ISO standard.
- **IS18 compliance without ISO 27001 is achievable** — and for many SME suppliers, it's the more practical path. IS18 doesn't require third-party certification; it requires evidence of a documented programme. A well-structured IS18 Compliance Statement, current security policy, risk register, and Essential Eight evidence pack satisfies the documentation requirements that IS18 flows down through supplier contracts.
- **ISO 27001 gives you more** — if you're also selling into the private sector, pursuing federal government work (which references the ISM more directly), or targeting enterprise clients, ISO 27001 is worth the additional investment. The controls overlap significantly, and an IS18-compliant programme is roughly 60–70% of the way to ISO 27001 certification already.

## What we found

The four-week engagement ran as a concurrent IS18 review and Essential Eight assessment.

### IS18 governance gaps

The MSP had reasonable operational security hygiene built up over years of government work. The security wasn't bad — the gaps were almost entirely in documentation and formal governance, not in technical controls. This is the pattern we see most often with government ICT suppliers: experienced practitioners who have been doing the right things operationally, but whose documentation hasn't kept pace with the formalisation that IS18 requires.

**Specific gaps identified:**

**Information security policy — outdated and unapproved.** The existing policy was five years old and referenced systems that no longer existed — including an on-premises Exchange server replaced three years ago and a legacy ticketing system. More critically, it had not been reviewed or approved by senior management in the current form. IS18 requires evidence of active senior management oversight; an outdated, unreviewed policy is a clear gap.

**Risk register — wrong methodology.** The risk register was asset-focused: it listed servers, network devices, and applications with associated risks. IS18 (aligned to ISO 27001) requires an information-risk-focused methodology: you start with information assets and the business processes they support, identify threats and vulnerabilities, assess likelihood and impact, and document treatment decisions. An asset register and an information risk register are different things.

**Supplier security assessment — absent entirely.** The MSP used several third-party tools and one subcontractor in their government engagements. None had been formally assessed. IS18 requires that suppliers managing government information assess their own sub-processors — this flows from the agency's obligations to the Queensland Government under IS18 section 3 (Information Security Risk Management). Informal familiarity with a vendor is not sufficient.

**Incident response plan — present but broken.** The plan existed, which was good. But it had not been tested in over two years, and contained escalation contact details for three staff members who had since left. For government engagements, the incident response plan also lacked the agency notification procedure — the specific steps for notifying the government agency client of a security incident within the required timeframe.

**Management review — no evidence.** IS18 requires periodic senior management review of the information security programme. There was no documented management review meeting, no minutes, no evidence of executive oversight. This is one of the first things a panel coordinator checks, and one of the easiest to fix.

### Essential Eight assessment

| Control | Assessed Maturity | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Application Control | ML0 | No policy, no enforcement — applications ran freely on government-connected endpoints |
| Patch Applications | ML1 | Patching occurring but no formal SLA, no compliance reporting, no evidence trail |
| Configure Office Macros | ML1 | Restricted for most users but exceptions existed without documented justification |
| User Application Hardening | ML0 | No formal baseline — browser extensions, Flash (legacy), and PowerShell unrestricted |
| Restrict Admin Privileges | ML1 | Some separation between admin and user accounts, but standing admin accounts with no JIT elevation |
| Patch Operating Systems | ML1 | Windows Update active, but inconsistent compliance across devices — no reporting dashboard |
| Multi-Factor Authentication | ML2 | Enforced for all users via Conditional Access — the strongest control in place |
| Regular Backups | ML1 | Backups running, but no tested restoration procedure and no documented RPO/RTO targets |

**Overall: ML1 across most controls, ML0 on two.** MFA was the standout — ML2 and genuinely well-configured. Application control and user application hardening were the most significant gaps, both at ML0 with no roadmap.

The panel submission required evidence of ML1 current state and a credible, documented pathway to ML2. The self-assessment that had been done 18 months earlier had rated Application Control as ML1 — the actual configuration showed this was not the case.

## What we delivered

Given the four-week deadline, the engagement was structured as assessment and roadmap — not full remediation. The output was designed to meet the panel submission requirement while giving the MSP a clear implementation path they could execute with their internal team.

**Weeks 1–2: Assessment and evidence gathering**

Full IS18 review and Essential Eight current-state assessment. Structured interviews with the IT Manager, Operations Lead, and CEO. Technical review of the M365 tenant configuration, Intune policies, Conditional Access rules, Defender for Endpoint settings, backup configuration, and existing documentation. This included configuration exports — not just self-reported status — to produce an evidence-based assessment rather than a questionnaire response.

**Week 3: Gap analysis and prioritisation**

IS18 gaps mapped to specific policy and procedure requirements, with a clear articulation of what each gap meant for the panel submission. Essential Eight gaps mapped to ASD ML2 criteria. Each gap was prioritised across three dimensions: panel submission criticality (could this gap by itself fail the submission?), risk impact (what's the actual security exposure?), and implementation effort (quick win / medium project / major project). This produced a prioritised action list where the team could see exactly which items needed to be completed before submission versus which could be scheduled into the 90-day roadmap.

**Week 4: Documentation uplift and roadmap delivery**

For the panel submission deadline, we delivered a complete documentation package:

- **Updated Information Security Policy** — rewritten to reflect the current environment (M365 Business Premium, Azure, on-premise AD), approved by the CEO, and structured with a defined two-year review cycle and management sign-off procedure built in
- **IS18 Compliance Statement** — a formal attestation document covering current compliance status against each IS18 requirement area, documented gaps, and a signed commitment to the remediation programme. Structured to address the specific questions Queensland Government panel coordinators ask
- **Essential Eight Current-State Report** — maturity assessed against ASD ML2 criteria with configuration evidence references (not self-reported), justified ML ratings for each control, and an honest account of the ML0 gaps with remediation context
- **90-Day ML2 Remediation Roadmap** — a control-by-control implementation plan for achieving ML2 across all eight strategies, with specific effort estimates, owner assignments, M365-native implementation guidance (WDAC via Intune, PIM configuration, ASR rules), and defined completion criteria
- **Incident Response Plan (updated)** — current staff contacts, defined agency notification procedure with timeframes, and a scheduled tabletop exercise for Month 2
- **Supplier Security Assessment Template** — a lightweight questionnaire and risk-rating framework for assessing third-party tools and subcontractors used in government engagements, pre-populated with the three current sub-processors
- **Risk Register (rebuilt)** — information-risk-focused methodology, 23 information assets scoped, likelihood/impact matrix, treatment plans documented with owners

**Panel submission:** The complete documentation package — IS18 Compliance Statement, Essential Eight evidence, updated security policy, and ML2 roadmap — was submitted to the Queensland Government panel coordinator three days before the deadline.

## The outcome

Panel renewal was approved. The MSP retained all three government agency contracts.

The panel coordinator's feedback was specific: the IS18 Compliance Statement and the Essential Eight Current-State Report gave them what they needed to evidence that the supplier had a credible security programme, even with acknowledged gaps. An honest, evidence-based submission with a committed remediation plan was more credible — and more approvable — than a clean-looking self-attestation that couldn't be verified.

**90-day implementation results**

The ML2 roadmap was subsequently implemented by the MSP's internal team with two checkpoint reviews from us at the 45-day and 90-day marks.

At 90 days: ML2 achieved across six of the eight controls.

- Application Control (WDAC via Intune) — deployed across all endpoints using a ring-based rollout (pilot → staff → all devices) over four weeks. Minor application compatibility issues resolved in Weeks 2 and 3 of rollout.
- Patch Applications — SLA formalised at 14 days for critical / 30 days for standard, automated compliance reporting via Intune dashboards, evidence cadence established
- Configure Office Macros — exceptions documented and reduced; remaining exceptions justified with business context and approved by the IT Manager
- User Application Hardening — browser baseline deployed via Intune ADMX profiles, PowerShell constrained language mode enabled for non-admin users, Attack Surface Reduction rules activated via Defender for Endpoint
- Restrict Admin Privileges — PIM deployed for all M365 and Azure admin roles, standing admin accounts eliminated for five roles. On-premises AD privileged access under review for Phase 2
- Patch Operating Systems — Windows Update for Business configured with compliance reporting, update ring cadence documented, non-compliant devices flagged via Intune

Two controls deferred:

- Application Control full coverage: WDAC deployed to 100% of managed endpoints, but the on-premises server estate required a separate project due to legacy application conflicts. Scheduled for Month 4.
- Regular Backups — backup running and documented, but the tested restoration drill was deferred due to a client change freeze window. Completed in Month 4 with documented RPO (4h) and RTO (8h).

**Secondary benefit: Security questionnaire efficiency**

This wasn't anticipated at engagement start, but it turned out to be one of the most practically valuable outcomes.

The MSP was receiving government security questionnaires — from agency clients and from new procurement opportunities — at a rate of roughly one per month. Each questionnaire was taking 4–6 hours to complete across the IT Manager and CEO because there was no centralised documentation to reference. Responses were inconsistent, and the team lacked confidence that the answers were accurate.

Post-engagement, the IS18 Compliance Statement, Essential Eight report, and updated security policy provided a pre-prepared, consistent, evidence-backed response to approximately 70% of the questions that appeared across all their government questionnaires. Subsequent questionnaire completion time: under one hour.

This compounds over time. Winning government work requires responding to questionnaires. Slow, inconsistent questionnaire responses communicate insecurity about your own security posture. Structured compliance documentation is a sales asset as much as it is a risk control.

## What this means for other Queensland Government ICT suppliers

If you're reading this because you supply ICT services to Queensland Government agencies, here's what we'd want you to take away.

**IS18 compliance is being actively enforced.** Panel renewals and new procurement rounds are increasingly using IS18 documentation requirements as a gate, not a formality. We're seeing this consistently across QLD Government panels and individual agency procurement. The "we've been doing this for years" argument doesn't satisfy a compliance requirement.

**The gap is almost always documentation, not controls.** Like this client, most experienced government ICT suppliers have reasonable operational security. The gaps that create compliance risk are almost always in the governance layer — outdated policies, undocumented processes, informal risk management, untested procedures. These are solvable quickly.

**Essential Eight ML2 is achievable for M365 environments in 90 days.** For an organisation already running Microsoft 365, the tools to achieve ML2 are already licensed — Intune for application control and patch management, Entra ID for PIM and MFA, Defender for Endpoint for ASR rules. The work is configuration and evidence capture, not platform procurement.

**An honest submission beats a vague one.** Panel coordinators are experienced. A clean-looking self-attestation with no supporting evidence is treated with scepticism. A documented current-state assessment that acknowledges gaps — paired with a credible, committed remediation plan — demonstrates that the supplier understands their environment and takes compliance seriously.
