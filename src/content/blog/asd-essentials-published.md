  <p>On 15 October 2026 ASD published the <strong>Essentials</strong>, the successor to the Essential Eight it announced in June. Here is what changed, what didn't, and what to do this quarter.</p>
</div>

<hr style="margin:36px 0;border:0;border-top:1px solid #DDD8CF" />

## What ASD published

- **Chapters for different environments.** The Essentials is built from environment-specific chapters. The current chapters cover **enterprise IT**, **cloud** and **operational technology**, and ASD says more can be added as needed.
- **One model in every chapter.** Each chapter has three layers: **principles** (the security outcomes to reach), **actions** (the work to get there) and **domains** (the business functions and assets the actions apply to).
- **No single maturity level.** The Essential Eight rated an organisation from Maturity Level Zero to Three. Under the Essentials, organisations report area by area (for example identity services or internet-facing systems) with effectiveness ratings backed by evidence. ASD says the implementation phases are not maturity ratings.

## What hasn't changed

- **Your current obligations.** Organisations should keep meeting existing regulatory obligations until regulators announce formal changes. Contracts, panels, insurers and the PSPF that name the Essential Eight still mean the Essential Eight.
- **The security work.** Patching, application control, MFA, restricting admin privileges and backups are still the core. The ACSC's head has said Essential Eight maturity work still counts toward the new framework.
- **The dates.** When the Essentials becomes mandatory has not been settled. In June ASD told iTnews it expected the Essential Eight to start deprecating about 12 months after the announcement and retire about 24 months after; those are estimates, not published dates.

## What to do now

1. **Keep your Essential Eight programme running.** If a customer or insurer asks for ML2 today, that is still the requirement.
2. **Check, don't assume.** ASD's guidance asks organisations to check their existing controls against the new framework. Map each control to the chapter that fits your environment before changing anything.
3. **Pick your chapters.** Most organisations need the enterprise IT chapter. If you run significant cloud workloads or OT, read those chapters too: they may fit better than the Essential Eight ever did.
4. **Organise evidence by area.** Reporting is now area by area with evidence-backed effectiveness ratings, so group your evidence (identity, internet-facing systems and so on) and show the control works, not just that it is configured.
5. **List who names the Essential Eight.** Note which contracts, panels and insurers reference it, so you know who to talk to when they move.
