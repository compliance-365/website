## The situation

A Brisbane-based managed services provider (MSP) with 45 staff had held a Queensland Government ICT supplier panel position for six years. Their primary revenue stream was managing IT infrastructure for three Queensland state government agencies.

In mid-2025, their panel coordinator notified them that panel renewal would require demonstrated compliance with the Queensland Government's Information Standard IS18 (Information Security) and evidence of Essential Eight maturity at ML1 or above, with a documented pathway to ML2.

They had six weeks before their renewal submission deadline. They had no formal IS18 compliance assessment, and their last Essential Eight self-assessment — conducted internally 18 months prior — was based on a spreadsheet that the team themselves acknowledged was optimistic.

The stakes were significant: the three government agency contracts represented approximately 65% of total revenue.

## What IS18 requires

Queensland Government Information Standard 18 (IS18) establishes information security requirements for Queensland Government agencies and, critically, for their ICT suppliers and contractors. IS18 requires suppliers to:

- Demonstrate an information security management programme aligned to ISO 27001 principles
- Implement the ASD Essential Eight mitigation strategies
- Maintain documented policies, risk management processes, and incident response capability
- Provide evidence of regular security assessments and management review

For an MSP managing government agency infrastructure, IS18 compliance isn't optional — it's a condition of being on the panel.

## What we found

The four-week engagement ran as a concurrent IS18 review and Essential Eight assessment.

**IS18 assessment findings:**

The MSP had reasonable operational security hygiene built up over years of government work, but the governance documentation had not kept pace with growth. Key gaps included:

- No current information security policy approved by senior management (the existing policy was five years old and referenced systems that no longer existed)
- Risk register was asset-focused rather than information-risk-focused — not aligned to IS18's requirement for a formal risk assessment methodology
- No supplier security assessment process — third-party software and subcontractors used in government engagements had never been formally assessed
- Incident response plan existed but had not been tested, and contained contact details for staff who had left the organisation
- No formal management review cycle — IS18 requires evidence of periodic senior management oversight of the security programme

**Essential Eight assessment findings:**

| Control | Assessed Maturity |
|---|---|
| Application Control | ML0 — no policy, no enforcement |
| Patch Applications | ML1 — patching occurring but no formal SLA or reporting |
| Configure Office Macros | ML1 — restricted for most users but not all |
| User Application Hardening | ML0 — no formal baseline |
| Restrict Admin Privileges | ML1 — some separation, no PIM, standing admin accounts |
| Patch Operating Systems | ML1 — Windows Update active, inconsistent compliance |
| Multi-Factor Authentication | ML2 — enforced for all users via Conditional Access |
| Regular Backups | ML1 — backups running, untested restoration |

Overall assessment: **ML1 across most controls, ML0 on two**. The panel submission required a credible ML2 pathway.

## What we delivered

Given the four-week deadline, the engagement was structured as assessment and roadmap — not full remediation. The output was designed to meet the panel submission requirement while giving the MSP a clear implementation path.

**Week 1–2: Assessment and evidence gathering**

Full IS18 and Essential Eight current-state assessment across all controls. Interviews with the IT Manager, Operations Lead, and CEO. Review of existing documentation, M365 tenant configuration, Intune policies, Conditional Access rules, and backup logs.

**Week 3: Gap analysis and prioritisation**

IS18 gaps mapped to specific policy and procedure requirements. Essential Eight gaps mapped to ASD ML2 criteria. Controls prioritised by: panel submission criticality (immediate), risk impact (high/medium/low), and implementation effort (quick/moderate/project).

**Week 4: Documentation uplift and roadmap delivery**

For the panel submission deadline, we delivered:

- **Updated Information Security Policy** — approved by the CEO, aligned to IS18 requirements, with a two-year review cycle built in
- **IS18 Compliance Statement** — a formal attestation document summarising current compliance status, identified gaps, and the remediation programme
- **Essential Eight Current-State Report** — maturity assessed against ASD criteria with evidence references and a justified ML rating for each control
- **90-Day ML2 Remediation Roadmap** — control-by-control plan for achieving ML2 across all eight strategies, with effort estimates, owner assignments, and M365-native implementation guidance
- **Supplier Security Assessment Template** — a lightweight questionnaire and risk-rating framework for assessing third parties used in government engagements

**Panel submission:** The completed IS18 compliance documentation, Essential Eight assessment report, and ML2 roadmap were submitted to the Queensland Government panel coordinator ahead of the deadline.

## The outcome

Panel renewal was approved. The MSP retained all three government agency contracts.

The 90-day ML2 roadmap was subsequently implemented by the MSP's internal team with two checkpoint reviews. By the end of the 90-day period, the MSP had achieved ML2 across six of the eight controls. Application control (WDAC deployment) and full backup restoration testing were deferred to Month 4 due to client change freeze windows.

The engagement also had a secondary benefit the client hadn't anticipated: the updated IS18 documentation and Essential Eight assessment gave the sales team credible, evidence-backed responses to government security questionnaires that previously took 4–6 hours each. Post-engagement, those questionnaires were completed in under an hour using the existing documentation package.
